## ⭐ Things I Liked

- **Variety of Flavors and Vegetables:** Working with a diverse set of ingredients made the cooking process engaging and the final dish far more dynamic. Dicing carrots, chopping green onions, and prepping peas allowed us to layer different textures throughout the dish. Combining aromatics like garlic and ginger with soy sauce and sesame oil created a well-rounded, savory flavor profile that elevated standard fried rice into a complete, balanced meal.
- **Frying Tofu for the First Time:** Learning how to properly prepare and fry tofu was the highlight of the lab. Prior to this class, I had never cooked with plant-based proteins. I learned how crucial it is to press out excess moisture before slicing the block into uniform cubes. Searing the tofu in a hot pan created a crisp, golden outer skin while keeping the inside tender. The tofu held its shape when tossed into the rice, and executing this technique successfully on my first attempt built a lot of confidence in my cooking abilities.

## ⚠️ Things I Disliked

- **Pacing and Cleanup Under Time Constraints:** The final stage of stir-frying required intense focus and fast movement. Because high-heat cooking leaves very little margin for error, adding ingredients, stirring constantly, and applying sauces happened in rapid succession. This fast pace made the end of the lab feel rushed. Managing the skillet while simultaneously clearing prep bowls, washing cutting boards, and sanitizing the workstation before the class bell rang added unnecessary stress.
- **Managing Moisture in Freshly Cooked Rice:** Using rice that was cooked during the same class period created challenges with texture. Fresh rice retains a high amount of surface steam and internal moisture. When added to the hot pan, the grains tended to stick together and clump instead of separating and browning evenly. Using cold, day-old rice from the refrigerator would have allowed the grains to crisp up individually rather than steam in the pan.

## 💡 Summary

The fried rice lab was a practical, hands-on lesson in culinary organization, heat management, and timing. The biggest takeaway was preparing all ingredients, sauces, and proteins completely before turning on the burner, since stir-frying moves too fast to prep on the fly. Learning to press and sear tofu demonstrated how pan temperature and patience create texture on soft proteins. Balancing salty and savory seasonings refined my ability to taste and adjust flavor as I cook. For future lab assignments, using pre-chilled rice and streamlining my prep work will help the final cooking and cleanup phases run much smoother.

## 📋 Ingredients

### Part 1: Rice Cooking
- 1 cup Long Grain White Rice (rinsed)
- 2 cups Cold Water
- 1 tbsp Butter
- ½ tsp Salt

### Part 2: Stir-Fry & Crispy Tofu
- 3 cups Cooked White Rice (chilled)
- ½ block Firm Tofu (approx. 200g, pressed and cubed)
- 2 Large Fresh Eggs (beaten)
- ½ medium Yellow Onion (finely diced)
- 1 medium Carrot (peeled & finely diced)
- ½ cup Frozen Green Peas
- 2 cloves Fresh Garlic (minced)
- 1 tsp Fresh Ginger (minced)
- 2–3 tbsp Vegetable Cooking Oil
- 2–3 tbsp Soy Sauce
- 1 tsp Toasted Sesame Oil
- 2 stalks Green Onions (sliced)
- 1 Fresh Orange (sliced into half-wheels for side plating)

---

## 🍳 Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Rinse & Cook Rice:** Rinse white rice until water is clear. Bring 2 cups water, 1 tbsp butter, and ½ tsp salt to boil. Add rice, cover, and simmer on low for 15–20 minutes until tender. Fluff and spread in a shallow bowl to cool.
2. **Mise en Place:** Dice onions and carrots. Mince garlic and ginger. Thaw green peas.
3. **Press & Sear Tofu:** Press firm tofu with paper towels for 5–10 minutes. Cube into ½-inch pieces. Heat 1 tbsp oil in a frying pan over medium-high heat and sear tofu undisturbed for 3–4 minutes per side until crisp and golden brown. Season with 1 tsp soy sauce and set aside.
4. **Sauté Aromatics:** In a deep wok, heat 1–2 tbsp oil over medium-high heat. Sauté minced garlic, ginger, and diced onions for 1–2 minutes until fragrant and translucent.
5. **Cook Vegetables:** Add diced carrots and peas; stir-fry for 3–4 minutes until tender-crisp.
6. **Scramble Egg:** Push veggies to the side. Pour beaten eggs onto the cleared surface; scramble softly and fold into the vegetables.
7. **Stir-Fry Rice:** Add chilled rice, breaking apart clumps with the spatula. Drizzle soy sauce and sesame oil along the wok perimeter. Toss rapidly for 2–3 minutes over high heat.
8. **Fold in Tofu:** Gently fold in crispy seared tofu cubes during the last 30 seconds.
9. **Creative Plating:** Mold hot fried rice into a heart silhouette on a presentation platter. Garnish with sliced green onions and a carrot peel ribbon. Arrange fresh orange wedges along the side for Ms. Levis.

## 📖 10 Cooking Terms (Member: Rashi)

1. **Simmer**: Simmer white rice covered on low heat for 15–20 minutes until water is absorbed.
2. **Dice**: Cut peeled carrots and onions into small, uniform cubes for even cooking.
3. **Mince**: Chop garlic and ginger finely to release aromatic oils throughout the oil.
4. **Press**: Press tofu between dry towels to remove moisture before frying.
5. **Sear**: Cook tofu cubes in hot oil until all surfaces develop a golden, crisp crust.
6. **Sauté**: Cook garlic, ginger, and onions in oil until translucent and fragrant.
7. **Scramble**: Cook beaten eggs in the hot wok until tender, soft curds form.
8. **Stir-Fry**: Continuous tossing and turning of rice, vegetables, and seasonings over high heat.
9. **Fold**: Gently combine crispy seared tofu into fried rice to preserve texture.
10. **Garnish**: Decorate finished plate with green onions, carrot curl, and fresh orange wedges.

## 🔬 Culinary Science Research & Facts (Member: Anika)

- **Cultural History:** Originated during the Sui Dynasty (581–618 CE) in Yangzhou, Jiangsu province as an anti-waste philosophy using leftover day-old grains.
- **Starch Retrogradation:** Cooking gelatinizes starches; chilling recrystallizes amylose (retrogradation), firming grain exteriors so they fry without turning gummy.
- **Tofu Searing Chemistry:** Pressing removes excess water so surface temperatures exceed 140°C, triggering the Maillard reaction for a crisp crust.
- **Wok Hei ("Breath of the Wok"):** Intense heat vaporizes aerosolized oil droplets in the wok draft, infusing subtle caramelized smokiness.
- **5 International Dishes:** Yangzhou Fried Rice, Kimchi Fried Rice (Korea), Nasi Goreng (Indonesia), Khao Phat (Thailand), Yakimeshi (Japan).
- **3 Fun Facts:** Eaten across all provinces in China; Day-old rice was historically a breakfast staple; Master wok chefs toss rice up to 3 times per second.
