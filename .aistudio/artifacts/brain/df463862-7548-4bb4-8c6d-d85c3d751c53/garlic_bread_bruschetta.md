## ⭐ Two Things I Liked
1. **Creative Freedom and Customization**: I really appreciated how much flexibility this recipe allowed for personal expression and trial. Having access to a variety of cheese options gave us room to experiment with different flavor combinations. Additionally, being able to choose how to slice the baguette and how to arrange the final components on the plate turned the preparation into an artistic process rather than just following a rigid set of steps.
2. **Rich Flavor and Complementary Textures**: The overall taste of the bruschetta was outstanding, particularly because of the rich, aromatic interplay between the garlic and the pesto. The savory, herbal notes of the pesto brought out the best in the bread. Texture was another highlight; the crispy, perfectly toasted crust of the baguette provided an ideal contrast to the soft, flavorful toppings.

---

## ⚠️ Two Things I Disliked
1. **Inefficient Dish Usage and Heavy Cleanup**: The primary drawback of this lab was the sheer volume of dishes we accumulated during the preparation process. It felt as though we were constantly at the sink washing utensils, small bowls, and prep tools. Streamlining the workflow, such as using the same prep bowls for multiple steps or reusing spatulas, would have saved a lot of time and kept the workstation much cleaner.
2. **Challenges with the Plating Tool**: Decorating the finished plate with the plating spoon proved to be quite frustrating. Because the pesto sauce was thick and textured, it did not flow smoothly or consistently off the spoon. Instead of creating a neat, precise drizzle or design, it tended to fall in uneven clumps, making it difficult to achieve the polished presentation I was aiming for.

---

## 📝 Summary & Lab Reflection
Overall, this lab was a fantastic experience and easily ranks as one of my favorite cooking sessions this term. I have a genuine passion for Italian cuisine, so working with classic savory ingredients was a treat. Having grown up eating and making bruschetta since I was seven years old, I have tried many variations over the years, but this particular version stands out as one of the very best I have ever made, largely thanks to my huge love for pesto sauce!

## 📋 Recipe & Instructions

### Garlic Bread Ingredients:
- 1 French Baguette (cut into even, medium-thickness slices)
- ¼ cup butter (softened at room temp)
- 2 garlic cloves (freshly minced)
- ½ tsp garlic powder
- ¼ tsp salt
- ½ tsp dried parsley

### Tomato Bruschetta Topping:
- 3 Roma tomatoes (washed, diced in small pieces)
- ½ tsp kosher salt
- 1 ½ garlic cloves (peeled and pressed)
- 3–4 fresh basil leaves (pat dry, cut small)
- 2 tbsp to 4 tbsp olive oil (pour 2 tbsp first, then add to taste)
- 1 tbsp balsamic vinegar
- Specialty garnish: Pesto drizzle (provided by Ms. Levis)

### Step-by-Step Lab Instructions:
1. Preheat the oven to 400°F.
2. In a small bowl, combine butter, garlic, garlic powder, salt, and dried parsley until well mixed.
3. Using a serrated knife, cut the bread into slices; not too thin but not too thick; aim for even slices.
4. Spread the cut sides of the bread with the garlic butter mixture. Place halves, cut side up, on a rimmed baking sheet.
5. Bake for 8 to 10 minutes or until golden and toasted.
6. Take out the bread and let it cool; place hot pan on top of the stove.
7. While garlic bread is baking, prepare the tomato bruschetta recipe: wash tomatoes, cut into small pieces, and set aside in a small bowl.
8. Peel garlic, press through garlic press, and place in a custard dish.
9. Gently wash basil and pat dry with a paper towel. Cut basil into small ribbon pieces.
10. In a large bowl mix tomatoes, garlic, and basil—do not mash the tomatoes.
11. Add balsamic vinegar and olive oil, combine with tomatoes, garlic, and basil. Add salt and pepper to taste.
12. Top warm garlic bread and plate an evaluation sample for Ms. Levis.
13. Complete station wash down, dish sanitation, and cupboard storage.
